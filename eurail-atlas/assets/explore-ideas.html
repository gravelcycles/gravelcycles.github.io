Station card

One summary per station: how much traffic runs through rather than ending there, how international it is, who operates it, when it goes quiet and how far its trains reach without a change.

Departures by hour, Wednesday

Operators by share of calls

First findings

  • München Hbf is a pure terminus in this data: every one of its 1,186 Wednesday calls starts or ends there. Köln Hbf (86%) and København H (89%) are almost all through traffic.
  • Genève is the most international big station: 57% of its calls are on trains that also run in France, almost all of them Léman Express.
  • București Nord never closes: its longest quiet spell is 27 minutes. Lyon Part-Dieu and Barcelona Sants are silent for about five hours.

Method and limits

Calls, operators, hours and quiet spells use Wednesday's timed runs. Through share counts calls where the train neither starts nor ends. Direct reach and countries use every journey of the week, including countries without public clock times. A train split across national feeds counts as separate pieces, so reach is a floor.

Last train home

Pick a city. Every other station is coloured by the latest time you can leave it and still arrive in that city before 01:00, changing trains if needed. Darker means you can stay out later.

Latest departure from other atlas cities

First findings

  • Bratislava to Vienna and Rotterdam to Amsterdam both still work after midnight: the last useful trains leave at 00:03 and 00:11.
  • From Barcelona, the last train that gets you to Madrid the same night leaves at 20:00. From Copenhagen to Stockholm it is 18:30.
  • To be home in Paris by 01:00 you must leave Brussels by 21:16 and London by 20:01 London time.

Method and limits

Reverse connection scan over Wednesday's timed runs: latest departure that reaches any station of the city by 01:00 local time, five-minute minimum change, walks under 700 m between stations. Times are local to the home city. Stations in Finland, Norway, Portugal, Croatia, Greece, Northern Ireland, most of Denmark and Hungary's domestic network have no public clock times and show no dots.

Day-trip finder

Leave after 07:00, spend at least four hours somewhere, and be home by 22:00. The map colours every station by how long you get there. The list picks the farthest well-served places that pass the test.

Farthest day trips with four hours or more

First findings

  • London and Lyon, 737 km apart, are a day trip from each other: four to four and a half hours on the ground either way, changing in Lille or Paris.
  • Paris has the widest day-trip range: 3,919 stations at least 300 km away still give you four hours, as far as Irun on the Spanish border (695 km).
  • Madrid reaches only 593 stations this way, but 417 of them are 300 km or more away. High-speed lines make it all or nothing.

Method and limits

Earliest arrival leaving the city at 07:00 (forward scan) and latest departure that is back by 22:00 (reverse scan) on Wednesday. Time on the ground is the gap between the two. Times are local to the starting city. Same limits as above: no metro or bus links, and countries without clock times are invisible.

Most timed stations

Median station by country

Index 1.0 means quick changes happen as often as chance. Countries with at least eight measured stations.

First findings

  • The most timed stations are small and mid-sized junctions, not big hubs. Gera Süd, Turnov, Česká Lípa and Aulendorf give a quick change about three times as often as chance.
  • Big stations score about 1.0. Zürich HB is 0.96: with so many trains in every direction, a quick connection does not need planning.
  • By country, the median Dutch station scores 1.4 and the median Slovak, Swiss and Czech station about 1.2. Britain (0.93), France (0.94) and Spain (0.95) are below chance.

Method and limits

Wednesday 06:00–22:00. A direction is the next stop plus line. Only feeders and departures running at most twice an hour count, and a train that continues is not a change. Chance is the same timetable with each direction shifted by 1 to 59 minutes. The wait histogram uses all such pairs up to an hour.

Longest overnight runs

First findings

  • 180 distinct overnight trains, 130 to 146 on any one night. Saturday is the quietest night.
  • Italy's Intercity Notte owns the long end: Lecce to Turin is 1,074 km in a straight line from first boarding to last stop.
  • Paris Austerlitz and Roma Termini are the busiest evening departure points, with seven overnight trains each, ahead of București Nord (6).

Method and limits

Local times at each station, any day of the week. Pieces of the same train from different national feeds are merged when one fits inside another. Overnight trains in Finland, Norway, Portugal, Croatia and Greece have no public times and are missing.

Countries without changing

For each station, the number of countries its direct trains call in, counting both directions and the station's own country. A measure of how far a platform reaches into Europe.

Most countries from one platform

First findings

  • Salzburg Hbf leads with 11 countries, from the Netherlands and Belgium to Croatia and Hungary.
  • Wien Hbf, Bruxelles-Midi and Dresden Hbf follow with 10 each. Dresden's reach comes from trains between Scandinavia, Prague and Budapest.

Method and limits

All journeys of the week, including countries without clock times. International trains are often split between national feeds, and a split piece only carries the countries of its own stops, so counts are a floor. Stations without a country are left out.

Country

Country cards

Six numbers per country, side by side. Bars are scaled to the highest country, and the sort control highlights one metric.

Wednesday timed runs. Average speed is straight-line distance over scheduled time between stops inside the country. Stop spacing is the median hop. Trip length is the median straight-line run of trains based in the country. Late and early is the share of departures between 22:00 and 06:00. Cross-border is the share of departures on trains that also call abroad. Clock-face is the share of departures in frequent groups whose minutes past the hour repeat. Denmark and Hungary are left out because only part of their clock data is public.

First findings

  • Switzerland is the most regular network (90% clock-face) and among the slowest (52 km/h). France and Sweden are fastest at about 80 km/h.
  • Luxembourg is the only country where most departures (54%) are on trains that also run abroad.
  • Romania is the slowest (49 km/h) and has no clock-face pattern at all.

Method and limits

Hops faster than 330 km/h are dropped as coordinate faults (67 on Wednesday). Countries with fewer than 3,000 timed departures are not shown. Slovenia's 100% clock-face rests on very few frequent groups.

Farthest in a day

Leave at 08:00 and take any trains. How far, in a straight line, can you be after 4, 8 and 12 hours?

4 hours8 hours12 hoursHover a dot for the station

First findings

  • Lyon reaches farthest in 12 hours: 1,314 km, to the west of Scotland via Paris and London. Amsterdam is next with 1,253 km, to the Barcelona suburbs.
  • London goes 1,037 km in 8 hours, to the Toulon coast via Paris. Eight hours from Vienna reaches only 617 km.
  • Bucharest gets 380 km in 12 hours. Its trains average under 50 km/h, and Hungary's domestic times are missing.

Method and limits

Earliest-arrival scan on Wednesday, 08:00 local departure, five-minute changes, short walks. Distance is from the city centre point to the farthest station reached. The farthest station is often a small halt just past a big city.

    Capital pull

    Share of fastest routes between two other hub cities of the same country that pass through the capital. Bern stands in for Switzerland.

    First findings

    • Frankfurt lies on 19% of all fastest hub-to-hub routes, more than any other city, but riders change there on only half of them.
    • London (19%) and Paris (15%) are pure change points: every route through them changes trains, because the lines meet at separate terminals.
    • Paris pulls 54% of fastest routes between other French hub cities. Berlin pulls under 6% of German ones, London 5% of British ones.

    Single train

    Train biography

    One train's Wednesday run, stop by stop: speed between stops along the route, time standing still at each stop, and the countries it passes.

    First findings

    • The Frecciarossa from Turin to Reggio Calabria covers 1,244 km in 11 h 35 and touches 240 km/h, yet spends 83 minutes standing at its 21 intermediate stops.
    • The Bernina Express averages 26 km/h over 120 km. The Podhalanin from Zakopane to Świnoujście stands for two hours in total across 41 stops.
    • LNER's London to Edinburgh run waits five minutes in total. The Eurostar to Amsterdam waits fourteen.

    Method and limits

    Speeds are straight-line distance over scheduled time between consecutive stops, so curving mountain lines read slow. Times are local at each station. Runs come from a single feed; the IR night train from Bucharest stops at the Hungarian border because Hungarian times are not public.

    Country

    Week in the life

    Scheduled trains on each day of the week as a share of the Tuesday–Thursday average, plus the share of train patterns that run every day.

    All journeys including countries without clock times. Sunday 1 November was All Saints' Day in much of Europe. Patterns in Britain and Hungary are filed per day of the week, so their every-day share reads near zero.

    First findings

    • Ireland, Slovenia and Croatia run less than half their midweek service on the Sunday holiday. Bulgaria, Lithuania and Romania barely change.
    • Romania runs fewer trains on Thursday and Friday (91%) than on Sunday (103%), which points to a calendar change in the feed that week.
    • Hungary shows almost no trains on Sunday and Estonia shows 29% more than midweek. Both are worth checking at source before shipping.

    What it adds

    The idea book already compared weekends with midweek. Laid out as a week strip it doubles as a data check: holiday cuts look smooth, feed gaps look like cliffs.

    Skipped ideas and data notes

    Skipped

    • Rare trains. Train numbers that run once or twice a week are mostly weekend and holiday variants of everyday services. At city-pair level only 26 pairs qualify, and most are pieces of daily international trains, such as the Hamburg–Budapest railjet, that appear whole in one feed on one day only.
    • Departure fan, terminus compass, operator territories, slack finder, rail deserts. Not attempted in this pass.

    Data notes

    • International trains are often split across national feeds with different day patterns, so whole-train views (rare trains, countries per platform, sleepers) need stitching by train number first.
    • Léman Express in Geneva shows hourly trains through Wednesday night, which keeps Genève from ever going quiet.
    • Hops over 330 km/h are dropped as coordinate faults. Distances are straight lines between calls.
